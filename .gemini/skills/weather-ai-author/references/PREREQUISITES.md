# Prerequisites for the Reader

To get the most out of this textbook, students and teachers should have the following foundational knowledge:

## 1. Programming (Python)
- **Core Python**: Functions, classes, and basic data structures.
- **Scientific Stack**: Proficiency with `numpy`, `pandas`, and especially `xarray` (the standard for multidimensional weather data).
- **Deep Learning Frameworks**: Familiarity with `PyTorch` or `TensorFlow/Keras` is essential for the "Operational Projects" section.

## 2. Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals
- **Concepts**: Understanding of neurons, activation functions, backpropagation, and loss functions (MSE, MAE).
- **AI Agents**: A basic understanding of how AI agents can automate data retrieval (e.g., using the `cdsapi` to fetch ERA5 data) and orchestrate model training.

## 3. Basic Science & Math
- **Calculus & Linear Algebra**: Necessary for understanding gradients and tensor operations.
- **Atmospheric Basics**: While we explain concepts from scratch, a general idea of "What is a high-pressure system?" or "How does the Earth rotate (Coriolis)?" is helpful.

## 4. Data Literarcy
- Understanding of coordinate systems (Lat/Lon/Level/Time) and the NetCDF/GRIB file formats commonly used in meteorology.
