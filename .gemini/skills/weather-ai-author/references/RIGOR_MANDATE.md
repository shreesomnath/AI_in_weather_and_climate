# High-Rigor Authoring: Visuals, Logic, and Verification

To produce a textbook of the highest academic caliber, all generations must adhere to this "Slow-Burn" rigor mandate.

## 1. The Research-First Mandate
*   **Multi-Source Verification**: Before drafting any technical section, perform at least three independent searches to cross-verify historical dates, mathematical constants, and SOTA performance metrics.
*   **Original Synthesis**: Never copy-paste or mirror existing textbook structures. Synthesize the "Physics" from masters like Holton and the "AI" from researchers like Goodfellow into a unique narrative.
*   **Prohibition of Em-Dashes**: Em-dashes (—) are strictly prohibited. Use commas, semicolons, or periods to maintain a clean, academic prose style.

## 2. Advanced Visual & Algorithmic Standards
*   **Pedagogical Tables**: Every chapter should include at least one comparative table (e.g., "NWP vs. AI: Scaling, Hardware, and Maintenance").
*   **Algorithmic Logic**: Algorithms must be provided in clean, documented pseudocode or Python (PyTorch style). Every line of code must have a "Physical Meaning" comment.
*   **Figure Narrative**: Figures (Mermaid or descriptions) must be integrated into the text. They should not stand alone; the surrounding prose must "walk the reader through" the visual.

## 3. Deliberate Pacing
*   **Turn-Based Depth**: Do not attempt to write an entire chapter in a single turn. Break chapters into 3-4 sections. After each section, stop to verify the citations and the mathematical flow.
*   **The "Senior Scientist" Review**: Before finalizing a draft, perform an internal "audit" of the logic. Ask: "Would a professor find this explanation too thin? Is there a real-world example missing?"

## 4. Typography Check
*   **Bold**: Terminology.
*   **Italic**: Variables and Cross-references.
*   **Font**: Explicitly target Times New Roman 12pt Justified (as a prompt instruction).
