# STEM Research & Review Mandate

To maintain the highest scientific integrity, every chapter must undergo a rigorous cycle of literature extraction and internal peer review.

## 1. Primary Source Extraction (Research)
*   **Journal Priority**: Prioritize findings from AMS (Journal of Atmospheric Sciences), RMetS (QJRMS), IEEE (Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing), and Nature/Science.
*   **Search Strategy**: Perform targeted searches for the "Current State of the Art" (SOTA). Do not rely on general knowledge; cite specific papers from the last 24-36 months for AI-related sections.
*   **Knowledge Synthesis**: Extract the mathematical "Proof of Concept" and the "Operational Reality" from these papers.

## 2. The Internal Peer Review Process
Before finalizing any chapter, the author must switch to a "Reviewer" persona.

### Reviewer Criteria:
1.  **Technical Completeness**: Is there a missing physical law? Is the math consistent with the prose?
2.  **Pedagogical Clarity**: Would an undergraduate student get lost in the jargon? Are the analogies effective or misleading?
3.  **Visual Integration**: Do the diagrams actually simplify the concept, or are they just decorative?
4.  **Logical Sequencing**: Does the section follow the "Origin -> Math -> Analogy -> AI" flow?

### The Iteration Loop:
1.  **Review**: Generate a private list of critical comments/gaps in the current draft.
2.  **Address**: Rewrite the section to explicitly close those gaps and answer the reviewer's silent questions.
3.  **Finalize**: Produce the "Addressed Version" as the final textbook chapter. (The private review notes must NEVER appear in the final file).

## 3. STEM Citation Standard
*   **BibTeX Integrity**: Ensure every paper mentioned has a full, correct citation in the bibliography.
*   **Linked Logic**: Scientific claims must be "tight"—meaning every claim of "superior performance" must be linked to a specific study or dataset comparison.
