# Computer Science Foundation: The "Theory First" Mandate

To produce a textbook that is technically complete and pedagogically sound, all AI-focused chapters must adhere to the **Theory First** mandate. We must explain the "Brain" before we explain the "Forecast."

## 1. The Structure of a Theoretical Foundation
Before discussing a meteorological application (like GraphCast), you must provide a rigorous Computer Science exploration of the underlying architecture.

### I. First Principles (The "What")
*   **The Conceptual Unit**: Define the fundamental unit of the architecture (e.g., the Neuron, the Graph Node, the Attention Query).
*   **The Logic of Information Flow**: Explain how data moves through the layers. What mathematical transformations are happening?
*   **Exhaustive Definitions**: Prohibit "mentioning" a term without a 2-3 paragraph explanation of its CS origin and necessity.

### II. Mathematical Rigor (The "How")
*   **Full Derivations**: Provide the step-by-step math for core mechanisms (e.g., the QKV dot-product in Attention, the Adjacency Matrix in GNNs).
*   **Computational Complexity**: Explain the Big-O complexity ($O(N^2)$, $O(N \log N)$) and why it matters for hardware (CPU/GPU).

### III. The Meteorological Bridge (The "Why")
*   **Analogy-to-Physics**: Only after the CS theory is established, relate the mechanism to a physical atmospheric process (e.g., "The Attention Score is the neural equivalent of a teleconnection link").
*   **Physical Grounding**: Explain why this specific CS architecture was chosen for weather science.

## 2. Formatting for Comprehensive Learning
*   **Theory Sections**: Dedicated, substantial sections (2-3 pages equivalent) focusing solely on the CS part of the AI.
*   **Application Sections**: Separate sections focusing on how the theory is implemented in projects like GraphCast or Pangu-Weather.
*   **Calm Explanation**: Avoid short, rapid-fire replies. Use a slow, deliberate academic pace that anticipates and answers the student's questions.
